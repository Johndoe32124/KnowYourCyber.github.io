How to Protect Yourself from Cyber Threats

Even if your data has been involved in a breach, there are practical steps you can take to reduce the risk of further harm. Cybersecurity doesn't have to be complicated - small habits can make a big difference.

Essential Security Practices

These six practices form the foundation of good cyber hygiene

01
PW

Use Strong, Unique Passwords

Create complex passwords with at least 12 characters including numbers, symbols, and mixed case. Never reuse passwords across different services.

Use a password manager →
02
2F

Enable Two-Factor Authentication

2FA adds a critical second layer of security. Use authenticator apps like Google Authenticator or Authy instead of SMS when possible.

Learn about 2FA →
03
NR

Never Reuse Passwords

If one service gets breached, attackers will try your credentials on other platforms. Unique passwords prevent credential stuffing attacks.

04
PH

Watch for Phishing Attempts

Verify sender email addresses, hover over links before clicking, and never download attachments from unknown sources.

05
MO

Monitor Account Activity

Regularly review login history, connected devices, and recent activity on important accounts like email and banking.

06
BK

Backup Important Data

Maintain offline backups of critical files. In case of ransomware, you can restore your data without paying criminals.

Urgent

What To Do In An Emergency

If you discover your information has been exposed in a data breach, follow these steps immediately

1

Change Your Passwords Immediately

Start with the affected account, then update any accounts using the same or similar passwords. Create strong, unique passwords for each service using a password manager.

Do this within 1 hour
2

Enable Two-Factor Authentication Everywhere

Two-factor authentication adds a critical layer of security to your accounts. Use an authenticator app instead of SMS when possible for better security.

Do this within 24 hours
3

Monitor Financial Accounts

Check bank and credit card statements for unauthorised transactions. Consider setting up transaction alerts for real-time monitoring of suspicious activity.

Do this immediately and daily
4

Freeze Your Credit

If sensitive data like Social Security numbers or national ID was exposed, contact major credit bureaus to place a freeze on your credit reports.

Do this within 48 hours
5

Report the Incident

File a report with your local authorities and the relevant data protection agency. This helps track patterns and may assist in investigations.

Do this within 1 week

Common Cyber Threats Explained

Understanding these threats helps you recognise and avoid them

PH

Phishing

Fraudulent attempts to obtain sensitive information by disguising as trustworthy entities via email or text. Often impersonates banks or popular services.

Learn more →
RW

Ransomware

Malware that encrypts your files and demands payment for their release. Often spreads through malicious email attachments or compromised websites.

Learn more →
TR

Trojan Horse

Malware disguised as legitimate software. Once installed, it can steal data, spy on activity, or create backdoors for other attacks. Named after the Greek myth.

Learn more →
CS

Credential Stuffing

Attackers use stolen passwords from one breach to access accounts on other platforms where you reused credentials. Automated bots try millions of combinations.

Learn more →
MI

Man-in-the-Middle

Attackers intercept communication between two parties to steal or manipulate data. Common on unsecured public Wi-Fi networks. Look for HTTPS to stay safe.

Learn more →
SS

SIM Swapping

Attackers trick mobile carriers into transferring your phone number to their device, bypassing SMS-based two-factor authentication.

Learn more →

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do first after a data breach?
Change your password for the affected service immediately. If you reuse that password elsewhere, change those accounts too. Then enable two-factor authentication on all important accounts.
How can I check if my data was leaked?
Use our database search above or visit Have I Been Pwned to check if your email appears in known breaches. Also monitor your credit reports and bank statements for unusual activity.
Is a password manager safe to use?
Yes, reputable password managers use strong encryption and are far safer than reusing passwords across sites. Choose a well-reviewed manager with zero-knowledge architecture.
What's the difference between 2FA and MFA?
Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) uses exactly two verification methods. Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) can use two or more. They're often used interchangeably, both adding security beyond just a password.
How does a Trojan horse infect my computer?
Trojans disguise themselves as legitimate software like game downloads, email attachments, or software updates. Always download software from official sources and be wary of unexpected attachments.
How can I protect against Man-in-the-Middle attacks?
Always look for HTTPS in your browser address bar, avoid public Wi-Fi for sensitive transactions, use a VPN, and never ignore certificate warning messages.
